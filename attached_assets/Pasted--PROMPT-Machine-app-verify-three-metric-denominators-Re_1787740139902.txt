# PROMPT — Machine app: verify three metric denominators

**Repository:** `fonnjain/Prayag-Plant-MC-Analysis` · deployed at `prayag-plant.com`
**Read-only first.** Report the findings before changing anything.

The planning app's monitoring panels (`prayag-pp.com`) display `rejection_pct`, `utilisation` and `output_efficiency` **exactly as this service returns them** — `/api/plant-live/summary` is a transparent proxy and recalculates nothing. Three of those figures do not reconcile on the planning side, so the question belongs here.

---

## 1 · Rejection % — the denominator may be wrong

### The evidence from the planning app

Four rows of the Plumbing Daily Output Log, August 2026:

| Date | Good | Reject | reject ÷ good | reject ÷ (good+reject) | **Displayed** |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 08-01 | 13,890 | 1,090 | **7.8%** | 7.3% | **7.8%** |
| 08-03 | 12,521 | 1,545 | **12.3%** | 11.0% | **12.3%** |
| 08-04 | 24,102 | 1,882 | **7.8%** | 7.2% | **7.8%** |
| 08-05 | 19,420 | 2,345 | **12.1%** | 10.8% | **12.1%** |

**Four of four match `reject ÷ good`.** If `total_count` holds good output rather than gross, the rate is overstated — by 1.3 points on 08-03.

### Where to look

`metrics.py:598`:

```python
_rej_denom = _reject_denom_sum if _reject_denom_sum > 0 else m.total_count
m.rejection_pct = _safe_div(m.reject_count, _rej_denom)
```

So the denominator is `total_count` for every plant except Tank. And `total_count` is documented at `metrics.py:139` as:

> *"net/good output **or** gross output before rejection. Set at Record construction from the plant contract."*

**The field is explicitly one or the other depending on the plant contract** — and `output_basis` records which. That is the likely cause.

### What to report

1. For **PIPE** and **PTMT**, what is `output_basis` — `net`, `gross`, `mixed` or unknown?
2. Dump one raw payload per plant showing `good_count`, `total_count`, `reject_count` and `rejection_pct` for a few machines, and state which of the two candidate formulas the reported value matches.
3. **Is `total_count` ever zero or absent** where `good_count` is populated? If it is, the fallback may have selected `good_count` rather than the contract choosing it — a different defect with a different fix.
4. If `output_basis` is `net` for these plants, confirm whether that is intended. A rejection rate on net output answers *"rejects per good piece"*, which is a valid measure but not the usual one — and it is not what the label implies.

**Do not change the formula before reporting.** If the basis is `net` by design, the fix is the label and the documentation, not the arithmetic.

## 2 · Utilisation — the implied capacity is not constant

Working back from the displayed figures, `run hours ÷ utilisation %`:

| Date | Run hrs | Util % | Implied capacity |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| 08-01 | 92 | 85.7% | **107.4 h** |
| 08-03 | 85 | 65.7% | **129.4 h** |
| 08-04 | 143 | 94.5% | **151.3 h** |
| 08-05 | 126 | 83.2% | **151.4 h** |

Daily capacity should be fixed — machines × available hours. It varies by **41%** across four days.

### The likely explanation, and why it needs confirming

`metrics.py:499` documents the denominator as:

> *"utilisation denominator: only hours from rows that ACTUALLY [ran]"*

`util_ideal` accumulates `r.shift_len_min` or `r.ideal_hours` **only for rows present in the data**. So a day where fewer machines reported produces a smaller denominator — and 08-04 and 08-05 both landing at ~151.4 h suggests those are the full-fleet days while the two lower ones had fewer machines.

**That may be correct behaviour** — it measures how well the *running* machines were used, rather than penalising a fleet for machines that were down. But it means daily utilisation figures are **not comparable across days**, and nothing on the screen says so.

### What to report

1. Confirm whether `util_ideal` varies with the number of reporting machines on each date.
2. For those four dates, report **how many machines contributed** and the summed ideal hours.
3. State whether a machine that did not run at all is excluded from the denominator or counted at zero — and whether that is deliberate.

## 3 · Output Efficiency — a small reconciliation gap

```
358,919 ÷ 594,720  =  60.35%      displayed: 60.0%
```

At one decimal place 60.35% renders as 60.4%, so this is not rounding.

`metrics.py:579`: `m.output_efficiency = _safe_div(eff_out, m.ideal_output)`

### What to report

Whether `eff_out` equals the displayed `TOTAL OUTPUT` (358,919) or something else — for instance gross rather than good output, or a figure that excludes some machines. And whether `ideal_output` equals the displayed 594,720. **The gap is 0.35 points, which is small enough to be a different numerator rather than a rounding rule.**

The planning app has a separate legacy path — `monitoring-calc.ts:189`, `computeMachineQuality()` — computing `rejectionKg ÷ totalOutputKg`, the gross denominator. **So two rejection figures exist across the two systems and may disagree.** Whichever proves correct, they should agree. Worth flagging in your report so it is reconciled once rather than twice.
