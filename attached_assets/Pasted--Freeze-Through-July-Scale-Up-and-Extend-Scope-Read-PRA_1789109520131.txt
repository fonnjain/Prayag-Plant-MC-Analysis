## PART B — report before building

Phase 1 freezes **daily production records only**. So a "frozen" month can still change in cost, because these remain live:

- wages, headcount, paid and actual hours (Segment Cost workbook)
- power, solar, tariff, contractor (Management Manual Entries)
- annual comparison figures
- whatever the Compound and PIPE mould-working reports read, since they bypass `get_daily_records()`

**A month is not really frozen until those are too.** Report what it would take.

### The problem that must be solved first

**Late-arriving data.** August wages still are not entered. Pipe's July wages arrived weeks after July closed. Power and contractor bills come later still.

So a month can be **complete in production and genuinely incomplete in cost**. Freezing it early would lock out figures that had not arrived.

Recommend one approach:

- **Two-stage freeze** — production freezes when output is complete; costs freeze separately once wages and bills land. Fully frozen only when both are done.
- **Readiness gate** — refuse to freeze until every expected component is present, naming what is missing.
- **Amendment path** — a frozen month can be reopened for one named component through an admin action, logged.

**State plainly what happens when a bill arrives for an already-frozen month. "It is lost" is not acceptable.**

### Also report

- **Ideal rates and business standards** are policy, not month-data. If a rate is corrected, should closed months pick it up? My instinct is yes — which would mean they must **not** be frozen. Recommend.
- The **specialised readers** that bypass `get_daily_records()` — snapshot them, or exclude them explicitly and document that those reports still call Sheets for a frozen month?

- No figure changes. No Sheets writes (**R-21**). No cache clearing outside what the mechanism does itself (**R-34**).
- **Do not batch freezes.** Each is a deliberate, reviewed action.
