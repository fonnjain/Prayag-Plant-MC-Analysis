## The competing hypotheses

Your report assumes the stored high-water (June 71, July 177) is correct and the source has since lost a record. That is **Hypothesis A**.

**Hypothesis B: the high-water is one too high, and the current count is correct.**

If a connection retry caused a single row to be parsed twice, the read would have produced **71 records instead of 70**, with inflated output — and that inflated count would have been persisted as the high-water. Every honest read since would then fail by exactly one.

Note what makes B plausible rather than speculative:

- The shortfall is **exactly one record** in both months — not a variable gap
- The output difference is **6,208 kg with 580 kg rejection** in June — the size of a single plausible machine-day, not a rounding artefact
- A partial or failed read produces **fewer** records and **less** output. The 71-record read had **more**. Corruption by omission cannot explain a higher count; **duplication can**
- The stored high-water records only the maximum count, **not the identity of the accepted rows** — your own report says so. So it cannot distinguish a genuine 71 from a double-counted 70

**A and B require opposite fixes.** A means restoring a deleted source row. B means correcting a phantom high-water. Doing the wrong one would either fabricate production or permanently lose a real record.
